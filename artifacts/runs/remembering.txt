Version 4, first attempt: the brain is not put back to rest between moves.

The same fly, the same weights, the only difference being how much of the brain
survives the gap between one move and the next. 50 games a row, whole-gradient,
on one 2060.

                     vs random        vs greedy      vs minimax 2
  reset each move    88% W  4% D     92-94% W       4% W 28% D 68% L
  carried whole      90% W  0% D     72% W  2% D    2% W  4% D 94% L

How much of it survives, against greedy:

  none (reset)   94% W
  a quarter      88% W
  a half         84% W
  three quarters 78% W
  all of it      72% W

Monotonic: every scrap of the previous move that survives costs it something.

It is not that the brain winds down. Over 40 moves carrying everything, the mean
membrane voltage settles from -56.16 mV to about -56.6 and stays there, and the
share of cells near threshold rises slightly (17.8% to 19.9%). The activity is
stable — it is simply a different operating point from the one the readout was
fitted to. The fly was taught to judge a board with a brain at rest, and we are
asking it to judge one with a brain still busy with the board before.

So memory cannot be bolted on. If a fly is to have it, it has to be taught with
it. That is the next version.
